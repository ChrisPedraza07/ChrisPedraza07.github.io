Character pipeline case study

T-Rex

A complete creature workflow built across Blender, Maya, and Substance 3D Painter—from the first high-resolution sculpt to an animation-ready character.

View the process ↓

The goal

One character, start to finish

I developed this T-Rex through the major stages of a production character pipeline. The project began as an expressive high-detail sculpt, then moved through clean topology and UV preparation before texturing. I finished by building a control rig designed for readable, animator-friendly posing.

01

Blender

Model & sculpt

I established the silhouette, proportions, and personality in Blender, then sculpted the large forms before layering in the creature’s scales, folds, and facial detail. Keeping the silhouette readable was especially important for the oversized head, compact arms, and powerful legs.

02

Maya & Blender

Remesh & UVs

I rebuilt the sculpt into an animation-ready quad mesh with deliberate edge flow around the eyes, mouth, shoulders, hips, and other deformation zones. After retopology, I cut and packed UV islands to give the body consistent texture space and clean seams.

03

Substance 3D Painter

Texturing

In Substance 3D Painter, I built a cool charcoal skin palette with a muted purple underside, warm claws, and a bright amber eye as the focal point. Baked surface detail and layered materials preserve the sculpted scale work while adding color variation and definition.

04

Maya

Rigging

I created a custom Maya rig with controls for the body, limbs, tail, and jaw. Custom attributes handle foot poses and toe spread, while blend shapes drive eyelid movement. The result is a practical character rig that supports both broad action poses and smaller facial beats.
